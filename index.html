Black-Scholes Option Greeks Dashboard

Prices European call and put options and calculates the five Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, Rho). I built the model in Python with NumPy and SciPy, then turned it into this live dashboard: enter any inputs below and every number and chart updates.

PythonNumPySciPyBlack-ScholesOption Greeks
Option type

European options, no dividends. Theta is shown per day, Vega per 1 volatility point and Rho per 1% rate move. Charts plot the selected option against the stock price; the amber line marks the spot price S and the grey line the strike X.

Historical Simulation VaR Dashboard

Estimates portfolio Value at Risk with the historical simulation method. It ranks past portfolio returns from worst to best and reads off the percentile for the chosen confidence level: VaR = −return at the (100 − confidence) percentile × portfolio value. My notebook runs this on 15 years of daily prices for ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, Kotak Bank, Axis Bank and the Nifty 50. This dashboard lets anyone load their own prices from Excel, then set the portfolio value, time horizon and confidence level, and choose which securities to hold and at what weights.

Distribution of returns and VaR lines

Method: daily log returns of the selected columns are combined using your chosen weights, summed over rolling windows of the chosen horizon, and the VaR is the percentile loss of those windows. Expected shortfall is the average loss in the tail beyond VaR. The sample data is simulated and is not real market prices. Historical VaR assumes the future looks like the past and can miss extreme events that are absent from the data.

Probability Distribution Dashboard

Two tools in one. First, upload a column of numbers from Excel and the dashboard finds which of the 14 core distributions describes it best, with a full set of statistics and charts. Second, explore each of the 14 distributions (Bernoulli, Binomial, Poisson, Discrete Uniform, Normal, Standard Normal, Continuous Uniform, Exponential, Chi-Square, Student’s t, F, Beta, Lognormal and Pareto) on its own graph, with its own mean, standard deviation and confidence-level sliders.

Each graph has its own mean, standard deviation and confidence-level sliders, plus its key statistics.

Notes: the KS distance is the largest gap between your data’s cumulative curve and the fitted one, and a p-value is not shown because the parameters are estimated from the same data. Files with more than 5,000 continuous values are fitted on an even sample. Student’s t is fitted with a location and scale. On the graphs below, the grey dashed line marks the mean and the amber band holds the chosen confidence level. Some sliders are locked because the distribution has no independent mean and standard deviation: Bernoulli, Poisson, Exponential and Chi-Square have one parameter, Student’s t has a fixed mean of 0, and the Standard Normal is fixed. Binomial and Discrete Uniform show the closest whole-number match. The simulated samples are generated in the browser and are not real data.

CAPM and Portfolio Performance Dashboard

An interactive workbench for the Capital Asset Pricing Model and the five core portfolio-management measures: Sharpe ratio, Treynor ratio, Jensen’s alpha, information ratio and tracking error. Use the formula calculator to see each measure and the Security and Capital Market Lines respond as you move the inputs, or upload your own data (Excel or CSV) to estimate beta and alpha by regression and score a portfolio against the market.

Binomial Tree Option Pricing

Price European and American calls and puts with the Cox-Ross-Rubinstein binomial model. Move the sliders for spot, strike, time, interest rate, volatility, dividend yield and the number of steps, and watch the tree, the price, the Greeks and the convergence towards Black-Scholes update. Small trees show every node, and early-exercise nodes are highlighted for American options.

Monte Carlo Simulation: Option Pricing and Portfolio Risk

Simulate thousands of random price paths to price European and Asian options, with antithetic variance reduction, standard errors and a convergence chart, and to estimate a portfolio’s Value at Risk and expected shortfall under normal or fat-tailed returns. Sliders control every input, and you can estimate the drift and volatility from your own Excel or CSV price file.
